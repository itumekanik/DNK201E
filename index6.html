📖 Theoretical Solution — Damped Spring-Mass Oscillator

🔹 Problem Statement

Determine the position as a function of time for a damped spring-mass oscillator. By using the free body diagram and Newton's second law, we can derive the equation of motion.

🔹 Equation of Motion

The sum of forces acting on the mass (spring force \( -kx \) and damping force \( -c\dot{x} \)) equals \( m\ddot{x} \):

$$ m \ddot{x} + c \dot{x} + k x = 0 $$

Dividing by mass \( m \), we rewrite this in standard form:

$$ \ddot{x} + 2\zeta\omega_n \dot{x} + \omega_n^2 x = 0 $$

where \( \omega_n = \sqrt{k/m} \) is the natural frequency, and \( \zeta = \frac{c}{2\sqrt{km}} \) is the damping ratio. The variable \( d = \zeta\omega_n = \frac{c}{2m} \) is often called the exponential decay rate.

🔹 Solution Regimes

The roots of the characteristic equation \( s^2 + 2\zeta\omega_n s + \omega_n^2 = 0 \) dictate the nature of the motion:

  • Underdamped (\( \zeta < 1 \)): Roots are complex conjugate. The system oscillates with exponential decay. $$x(t) = e^{-\zeta\omega_n t} \left( A \sin(\omega_d t) + B \cos(\omega_d t) \right)$$ where \( \omega_d = \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} \) is the damped natural frequency.
  • Critically Damped (\( \zeta = 1 \)): Roots are real and repeated. The system returns to equilibrium as fast as possible without oscillating. $$x(t) = (A + Bt) e^{-\omega_n t}$$
  • Overdamped (\( \zeta > 1 \)): Roots are real and distinct. The system returns to equilibrium slowly without oscillating. $$x(t) = A e^{s_1 t} + B e^{s_2 t}$$

🔹 Initial Conditions

Constants A and B are found using \( x(0) = x_0 \) and \( \dot{x}(0) = v_0 \). For an underdamped system, this yields:

$$ A = \frac{v_0 + \zeta\omega_n x_0}{\omega_d}, \quad B = x_0 $$
