📖 Theory — Perfectly Plastic Impact & Momentum Conservation

🔹 Problem Statement (P16.48)

A 400-kg satellite S travels at 7 km/s horizontally. A 1-kg meteor M traveling at 12 km/s at 45° above the negative x-axis strikes the satellite. The meteor is embedded in the satellite (perfectly plastic impact). Find the magnitude V of the combined velocity and angle β between V and the original satellite path.

🔹 Key Principle — Conservation of Linear Momentum

For a perfectly plastic (inelastic) impact with no significant external impulsive forces during the collision, the total linear momentum of the system is conserved:

$$\sum \vec{p}_{before} = \sum \vec{p}_{after}$$
$$(m_S \vec{v}_S) + (m_M \vec{v}_M) = (m_S + m_M)\vec{V}$$

🔹 Setting Up Coordinates

Take +x in the direction of the satellite's motion, +y upward.

Satellite velocity: $\vec{v}_S = v_S\,\hat{i}$

Meteor velocity (from lower-right, going upper-left at angle φ above −x): $$\vec{v}_M = -v_M\cos\varphi\,\hat{i} + v_M\sin\varphi\,\hat{j}$$

🔹 Momentum Equations

x-component

$$m_S v_S + m_M(-v_M\cos\varphi) = (m_S+m_M)V_x$$

$$V_x = \frac{m_S v_S - m_M v_M\cos\varphi}{m_S+m_M}$$

y-component

$$0 + m_M(v_M\sin\varphi) = (m_S+m_M)V_y$$

$$V_y = \frac{m_M v_M\sin\varphi}{m_S+m_M}$$

🔹 Numerical Solution

With m_S = 400 kg, m_M = 1 kg, v_S = 7 km/s, v_M = 12 km/s, φ = 45°:

$$V_x = \frac{400(7) + 1(-12\cos 45°)}{401} = \frac{2800 - 8.485}{401} \approx 6.9689\text{ km/s}$$
$$V_y = \frac{1(12\sin 45°)}{401} = \frac{8.485}{401} \approx 0.02116\text{ km/s}$$
$$|V| = \sqrt{V_x^2 + V_y^2} \approx 6.9690\text{ km/s}$$
β = arctan(V_y / V_x) = arctan(0.02116 / 6.9689) ≈ 0.174°

The small angle β reflects that the very massive satellite barely deflects — only the tiny fraction (1/401) of the meteor's transverse momentum matters.

🔹 Energy Consideration

This is an inelastic impact — kinetic energy is not conserved. The energy lost is converted to heat, deformation, and sound in the collision.

$$\Delta KE = \frac{1}{2}m_S v_S^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_M v_M^2 - \frac{1}{2}(m_S+m_M)V^2$$
